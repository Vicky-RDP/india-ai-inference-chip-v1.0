# India AI Inference Chip v1.0 — Brand Identity v1.0

**Status:** Working source of truth  
**Date:** 26 August 2026  
**Short name:** IAIC  
**Descriptor:** Open inference silicon from India

## 1. Brand foundation

### The idea

India AI Inference Chip is an open-source beginning for an India-originated AI inference stack: silicon, RTL, software, tools, benchmarks, and the people who make them useful.

### Brand promise

Make efficient inference more open, local, reproducible, and buildable.

### Positioning

For engineers, researchers, students, startups, universities, and institutions who want more control over the intelligence inside real products, IAIC is the open India-originated inference project that publishes the work from verified RTL toward usable silicon. It is a public engineering effort, not a finished commercial chip or a flag-waving claim.

### The one thing to remember

**India-originated inference silicon, built in public with the world.**

### Personality

- **Precise:** claims are earned with tests, benchmarks, and source.
- **Open:** people can inspect the work and find a way to contribute.
- **Grounded:** start small, name the limits, keep going.
- **Ambitious:** the first dot product is a beginning, not the destination.
- **Indian, not insular:** India is the point of origin; participation is global.

## 2. Messaging system

### Message hierarchy

1. **What:** Open-source AI inference silicon and software from India.
2. **Why:** Inference is where AI meets the physical world, so access to the stack matters.
3. **How:** Start with a small, verifiable INT8 core and build outward in public.
4. **Invitation:** Contribute hardware, verification, software, benchmarks, documentation, or ideas.

### Approved message lines

- **Primary:** India needs its own AI inference chip. Let’s build it in the open.
- **Short:** Open inference silicon from India.
- **Community:** Built in India. Built with the world.
- **Technical:** From open RTL to reproducible inference.
- **Devanagari accent:** `भारत से · दुनिया के लिए` — From India, for the world.

### Claims discipline

Say “open-source beginning,” “project,” “prototype,” or “path to silicon” until evidence supports a stronger claim. Do not say “India’s fastest,” “production-ready,” “sovereign chip,” or “world-class performance” without a public benchmark and clear comparison context.

## 3. Voice and writing

### Voice

Write like a generous hardware lead who shows the testbench. Use short sentences, concrete nouns, and active verbs. Be proud of the origin without making nationality a substitute for evidence.

### Do

- “The first milestone is a portable signed INT8 dot-product engine.”
- “Run the reference model. Read the RTL. Open an issue.”
- “The result is early. The work is public.”
- Explain what is true now and what comes next.

### Avoid

- Empty superlatives: revolutionary, world-class, game-changing.
- Nationalistic abstraction without a build path.
- Vague community language: “join the movement” without a concrete first action.
- Overclaiming sovereignty before there is a working stack.

### Editorial rules

- Use “India AI Inference Chip v1.0” on first mention; “IAIC” after that.
- Use sentence case for headings.
- Use `INT8`, `RISC-V`, `RTL`, `FPGA`, and `ASIC` exactly as written.
- Prefer “from India” or “India-originated” to “made in India” when discussing the whole open ecosystem.
- When using Hindi or another Indian language, include the English meaning nearby. Do not imply Hindi represents every Indian contributor.

## 4. Visual identity

### Concept: Circuit & Chakra

The identity joins two systems: the chip grid represents engineering, repeatability, and scale; the chakra represents motion, public purpose, and the Indian flag without turning the brand into a literal flag. Together they create a signal that feels specific to India and credible in a technical room.

### Logo and mark

The primary mark is the **Signal Chakra**: a dark chip frame, a reduced wheel, and three horizontal signal rails.

- Use the mark with the full wordmark on first-touch brand surfaces.
- Use the mark alone for favicon, avatar, repository badge, and small hardware labels.
- Keep clear space equal to the width of one outer chip pin on all sides.
- Minimum digital size: 20px for the mark, 120px for the full wordmark.
- Do not stretch, rotate, bevel, add a gradient, or place the mark over busy imagery.
- Do not use the Ashoka Chakra as a generic decorative background at large opacity; it is a recognition signal.

### Colour palette

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Ashoka Navy | `#000080` | Heritage signal, links, focus, technical emphasis |
| Chip Ink | `#071A2B` | Text, dark canvas, primary CTA |
| Bharat Saffron | `#FF9933` | Action, live signal, emphasis |
| Bharat Green | `#138808` | Progress, verified, shipped |
| Chakra White | `#FFFFFF` | Flag reference, dark-surface text |
| Ivory | `#FBF8F1` | Primary light canvas |
| Sand | `#F1EBDD` | Secondary surface |
| Terracotta | `#B85C38` | Editorial warmth, limited accent |
| Turmeric | `#E0A52B` | Annotation and caution |
| Neem | `#526A4A` | Muted edge/earth accent |

### Colour behaviour

Use Chip Ink and Ivory for most of the surface area. Let saffron and green appear as small, meaningful signals. A common composition is: Ink canvas + Ivory type + Saffron action + Green status + Navy link. On light surfaces, always preserve a visible rule around the white band of the tricolour.

### Typography

- **Primary:** Hind. Use it for English and Devanagari. Its Indian type design gives the system a local voice without sacrificing technical clarity.
- **Technical:** IBM Plex Mono. Use for version numbers, file paths, benchmark values, status labels, and code-adjacent UI.
- **Optional editorial accent:** Tiro Devanagari Hindi for a short quote or campaign line, never for dense UI.

Recommended pairings:

```css
--font-display: "Hind", sans-serif;
--font-body: "Hind", sans-serif;
--font-mono: "IBM Plex Mono", monospace;
```

### Graphic language

- 4px baseline grid, 1px rules, small radii, and generous editorial whitespace.
- Use square chip modules, signal bars, port/pin marks, and 12/16-spoke radial geometry.
- Diagrams should look publishable: label inputs, outputs, versions, and assumptions.
- The tricolour is a signal line or edge detail, not a full-background fill.

### Photography and illustration

Prefer:

- FPGA boards, chip packages, lab benches, oscilloscopes, notebooks, and hands-on making.
- Indian universities, startups, workshops, factories, farms, and real deployment contexts.
- Diagrams that connect perception → compute → action.

Avoid:

- Generic AI humanoids, glowing blue brains, stock flags, or empty server-room imagery.
- Decorative circuit-board textures with no information value.
- Overly glossy renders that imply a finished product before the hardware exists.

## 5. Channel rules

### Website

Lead with the mission, then show the current technical milestone. Keep the CTA concrete: read the architecture, run the model, open GitHub, or contribute.

### GitHub

Use the Signal Chakra as avatar/favicon. Use Chip Ink, Ivory, and Saffron for generated visuals. Keep repository copy factual and link claims to source, tests, or benchmarks.

### Social cards

Use one strong line, one chip/chakra motif, and one tricolour rule. Place `भारत से · दुनिया के लिए` only when it improves the story; never as a slogan pasted on every asset.

### Presentations

Use dark Ink slides for technical narrative, Ivory slides for people and invitation, and Saffron/Green only for calls-to-action and status. Keep code and benchmark slides visually quiet.

## 6. Accessibility and inclusion

- Never use saffron, green, or turmeric as the only indicator of state.
- Pair colour with text, shape, icon, or position.
- Maintain WCAG AA contrast for body text and controls.
- Keep Devanagari secondary to meaning, not a requirement for participation.
- When localising, use a script-appropriate companion font and preserve the same hierarchy and token roles.

## 7. Quick reference

When in doubt, ask:

1. Does this help someone understand the work or join it?
2. Is the Indian reference specific and restrained?
3. Can we support the claim with public evidence?
4. Does the piece feel like open engineering, not generic AI marketing?
